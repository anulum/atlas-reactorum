# Prioritised reactor-library gaps

This audit identifies acquisition and catalog priorities. It does not claim exhaustive completeness.

## Priority 1 — foundational gaps

1. **Official data and validation layer.** The main catalog now includes live, access-aware pointers for IAEA PRIS, ARIS, INIS, ENDF/EXFOR, FENDL/ALADDIN and SANIS, plus controlled-distribution notes for ICSBEP and IRPhE. Benchmark-level metadata and locally reusable quantitative subsets remain thinner than the document collection.
2. **BWR and complete water-reactor coverage.** The NRC BWR reactor-concepts manual is now downloaded and catalogued. The IAEA advanced large-water-cooled-reactor comparison remains an acquisition target.
3. **Fusion power-plant integration.** Plasma concepts are covered better than reactor heat extraction, blankets, tritium processing, remote maintenance, availability, licensing, and balance of plant. EU DEMO reactor-level studies and FENDL should be first additions.
4. **Multiphase chemical reactors.** Bubble-column, airlift, moving-bed, monolith, oscillatory-baffled, and rotating-packed-bed families need explicit coverage; trickle-bed and slurry coverage remains thin.
5. **Bioreactor breadth.** Add photobioreactors, perfusion/mammalian-cell reactors, membrane bioreactors, gas-fermentation reactors, immobilized-enzyme reactors, biofilm reactors, and solid-state fermentation scale-up.
6. **Process-safety evidence.** The CSB reactive-chemical process-safety study is now downloaded and catalogued. Searchable incident-level records and deeper coverage of calorimetry, relief sizing, inherently safer design, HAZOP/LOPA, management of change, and human factors remain open.

## Priority 2 — important current literature

1. **2020-2026 fission engineering:** maritime/floating reactors, microreactor licensing, transient reviews, safeguards/security-by-design, advanced manufacturing, digital I&C, decommissioning-by-design, and waste/fuel-cycle implications.
2. **Fusion diversity:** magnetic mirrors, reversed-field pinch, levitated dipoles, IEC/Polywell, dense plasma focus, direct-drive laser systems, pulsed magnetic targets, and direct energy conversion. Each needs a critical source, not only a proponent paper.
3. **Electrochemical reactors:** current-density scale-up, bubbles/two-phase transport, gas-diffusion electrodes, stack engineering, degradation, redox-flow reactors, electrodialysis, and electro-membrane reactors.
4. **Intensified and field-coupled reactors:** microwave, ultrasound, solar thermochemical, photoelectrochemical, plasma-catalytic, spinning-disc, and additive-manufactured/structured reactors.
5. **Flexible operation:** transient fixed-bed and electrochemical reactor models for renewable-powered plants; dynamic safety and control under start-stop operation.
6. **Hybrid energy systems:** nuclear process heat, hydrogen, desalination, district heat, thermal storage, chemical storage, and electricity-market operation need component-level and system-level sources.

## Priority 3 — speculative/emerging, segregated from validated systems

1. **Commercial fusion registry.** Maintain the separate candidate registry with status history, aliases, experimental devices, peer-reviewed publications, patents, national-program links, and explicit separation of claimed versus independently observed milestones.
2. **Aneutronic and non-equilibrium fusion.** Pair every p-B11, IEC, beam, or nonthermal concept with loss/radiation and recirculating-power analyses such as Rider's critical constraints. A fusion event or neutron yield is not net energy.
3. **LENR and lattice-confined systems.** Keep electrochemistry-only LENR, externally driven lattice-confinement fusion, beam-target screening, pyroelectric fusion, muon-catalyzed fusion, and cavitation claims in distinct categories. Pair new primary claims with the existing DOE, Google, and Callisto null/critical literature.
4. **Autonomous/self-driving reactors.** Treat AI-controlled laboratories as an experimentation and controls layer, not a new validated reactor physics class. Require reproducibility, calibration, interlock, cybersecurity, and safe-failure evidence.
5. **Company-only concepts and stealth projects.** Catalog as leads with dated snapshots. Do not infer reactor feasibility from funding, patents, partnerships, rendering images, power-purchase agreements, or schedules.

## Recommended acquisition order

1. Reusable quantitative subsets and the remaining large-water-reactor comparison; the official database pointers and BWR manual are now catalogued.
2. OA EU DEMO, fusion-device survey, multiphase chemical, photobioreactor, gas-fermentation, and rotating-packed-bed reviews.
3. CSB incident corpus and reactor transient review.
4. Paywalled cornerstone books only as metadata unless the user has lawful institutional access.
5. Speculative/company material last, in a visibly separate evidence tier with counter-sources.

## Maintenance notes

- Re-run DOI/URL deduplication before merging candidates into section catalogs.
- Recheck OA status at acquisition time; it can change.
- Refresh the company registry at least annually and after major acquisitions, closures, renamings, or public test campaigns.
- Preserve failed replications and critical reviews when pruning; negative evidence is essential to an honest reactor library.
